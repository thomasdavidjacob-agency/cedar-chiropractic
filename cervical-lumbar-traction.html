Cervical & Lumbar Traction

Gentle, targeted traction that eases pressure on the discs and nerves of the neck and lower back.

Home / Services / Cervical & Lumbar Traction

What traction is

Traction is a gentle, sustained pull along the length of the spine. It slightly opens the space between vertebrae, which may relieve pressure on the discs and on nerves that are being pinched or irritated. We use it in two areas:

  • Cervical traction for the neck — a non-surgical option for neck pain and cervical disc herniations.
  • Lumbar traction for the lower back — decompressive traction that relieves pressure on lumbar discs and may calm sciatica and disc-related pain without surgery.

What it’s commonly used for

  • Neck pain, including pain from a cervical disc herniation
  • Pain, numbness, or tingling that travels into the arm or leg from nerve pressure
  • Sciatica and lumbar disc-related pain
  • Stiffness and compression after a whiplash-type injury

What a visit feels like

We start with an exam so we know exactly which area to treat. You’re positioned comfortably and the pull is applied gradually and held — most people feel a mild stretch, and many find it relaxing. Nothing about it should be sharp or painful. We check in as we go, and you can ask us to ease off or stop at any point.

Traction is usually one part of a visit rather than the whole thing; it pairs naturally with adjustments and soft-tissue work.

Who it may not be right for

Traction may not be appropriate with certain conditions — for example a recent fracture, significant osteoporosis, joint instability, recent spinal surgery, pregnancy (for lumbar traction), or some other medical conditions. We screen for these during your exam. If traction isn’t a good fit, we’ll tell you why and recommend another approach or a referral.

How it fits with other therapies

Traction is often combined with:

  • Spinal DecompressionA closely related therapy for disc pressure
  • Spinal AdjustmentsRestores motion in stiff spinal joints
  • Rehab & Corrective ExerciseBuilds stability so relief lasts

Common Questions

Cervical & Lumbar Traction: questions we hear

What’s the difference between cervical and lumbar traction?

Cervical traction treats the neck; lumbar traction treats the lower back. The idea is the same — a gentle, sustained stretch that eases pressure on discs and nerves — applied to whichever area your exam points to.

Does traction hurt?

It shouldn’t. Most people feel a mild stretch and many find it relaxing. If anything feels sharp or wrong, tell us and we’ll ease off or stop.

Can traction help sciatica?

It may. When sciatica comes from pressure on a nerve in the lower back, lumbar traction aims to relieve some of that pressure. Your exam tells us whether that’s the likely cause in your case.

Is traction the same as spinal decompression?

They’re closely related — both gently stretch the spine to relieve disc and nerve pressure. We’ll recommend the one that suits your condition.
