Desk-Job Neck Pain: Why It Happens and What May Help

By the Cedar Chiropractic Team · 5 min read · Back to blog

It usually starts around 2 p.m. A dull pull at the base of your neck, one shoulder creeping toward your ear, and a head that feels heavier than it did this morning. If you work at a desk in Portland or Milwaukie, or from a kitchen table in Oak Grove, you are in very good company. Desk-job neck pain is common, and the reasons behind it are usually ordinary ones.

The head is heavy, and it likes to drift forward

Your head sits on top of a small stack of joints, held up by muscles that work all day. When you lean toward a screen, the head moves forward of the shoulders and those muscles have to work harder to hold it up. After hours of that, they tighten, tire, and ache. The joints in between can get stiff from staying in one position, and the shoulders often rise and round forward to match.

It is not that sitting is bad. It is that sitting in the same position, without a break, asks the same tissues to do the same job for hours.

Common desk culprits

  • A screen that is too low. A laptop on a table is the classic one, since looking down tips the head forward.
  • A screen off to one side. Holding a twist for hours loads one side of the neck.
  • The phone wedged against your shoulder during calls.
  • Reaching for the mouse with a raised, tense shoulder.
  • Long stretches without moving. Even a good setup gets uncomfortable if you never change position.

When it is more than a stiff neck

Most desk-related neck pain is a muscle and joint issue. But some symptoms need a medical look before anything else. Seek urgent care, or call 911 for severe or sudden symptoms, if you have:

  • Numbness, tingling, or weakness in an arm or hand that is new or getting worse
  • Loss of bladder or bowel control, or trouble walking
  • Neck pain after a fall, a collision, or another injury
  • Neck pain with fever, or with a sudden severe headache
  • Chest pain, shortness of breath, or pain that spreads into the jaw or arm

Pain that travels down the arm can also come from a disc or a pinched nerve, which is a different conversation. Our guide to cervical disc herniation covers those signs. If the pain began after a crash, start with rear-end collisions and neck injuries and our auto injury page.

What a visit looks like

If you are searching for a neck pain chiropractor near Milwaukie, here is what to expect. We ask about your work, your setup, and when the pain shows up. We look at how your neck and upper back move, check for tender and restricted areas, and screen for anything that points to a nerve being involved. If something falls outside what we do, we will tell you and refer you on. Our new patient page has the details on booking, and our insurance page covers coverage questions.

From the front desk: Tell us what a normal workday looks like, including how many hours you sit and where your screen is. It tells us as much as the exam does.

How chiropractic care may help

After an exam, a plan for desk-related neck pain may include spinal adjustments to help stiff joints move more freely, ultrasound and electrical muscle stimulation to calm tight muscles, cervical traction in some cases, and rehab and corrective exercises to build the strength that supports your neck through a long day. Care like this may help, but results vary from person to person and we do not promise a particular outcome. Why exercises matter is covered in why rehab exercises are the key to lasting relief.

What you can start today

Small changes cost nothing. Raise your screen toward eye level, take a short break to stand and move every so often, and let your shoulders drop when you notice them creeping up. We put the details together in posture at your desk: small fixes that prevent big pain. If your neck pain also brings headaches, see the neck-headache connection. And if you cannot tell whether it is time to come in, when to see a chiropractor vs. wait it out can help you decide.
