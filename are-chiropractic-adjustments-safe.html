Are Chiropractic Adjustments Safe? What to Expect on Visit One

By the Cedar Chiropractic Team · 5 min read · Back to blog

Almost everyone has heard the sound. A sharp pop, a wince, a friend saying "my cousin went once and never again." So it is no surprise that "are chiropractic adjustments safe?" is one of the questions we hear most from new patients around Milwaukie and Oregon City. Here is a straight answer, including the parts that are not entirely comforting.

What an adjustment actually is

An adjustment is a quick, controlled movement applied to a joint that is not moving as freely as it should. That is the whole idea. The popping sound, when there is one, is gas shifting in the fluid around the joint, much like cracking a knuckle. It is not bones grinding, and plenty of adjustments make no noise at all.

It does not have to be forceful, either. Gentler low-force techniques exist, and you can tell us which you prefer. Our spinal adjustments page goes into the options.

What most people feel afterward

Most patients describe adjustments as comfortable, even a relief. Some feel mild soreness for a day or so afterward, a bit like the day after a new workout. Others feel a little tired or loose. That is usually the body reacting to joints moving in ways they had stopped moving, and it tends to fade quickly. If it does not, or if it is getting worse, call us rather than waiting.

The rare risks, said plainly

Serious complications from chiropractic care are considered rare, but not impossible, and you deserve to hear that. The one people ask about most is the neck. There have been reports of tears in the arteries that run through the neck after neck manipulation, which can lead to a stroke. Researchers still debate how often that is caused by the adjustment itself and how often it is a problem already underway, since neck pain and headache can be early signs of an artery problem. We are not going to give you a number we cannot stand behind.

That uncertainty is a reason for care in the exam, not a reason to panic. It is also why we want to know your whole story before anything gets touched.

Worth knowing: an adjustment is not the only tool. Some visits focus on traction, ultrasound and e-stim, or rehab exercises, and your plan is based on your exam, not a script.

If you are nervous

Say so. It is common, and it changes how a visit goes. You can ask us to explain each step before it happens, to start gently, or to stop. Tension makes everything harder, including the exam, so talking it through is part of the care.

What we need to hear from you

The single best safety measure is a complete history. Before your first adjustment, tell us about:

  • Any recent injury, crash, or fall, even if it seemed minor
  • Sudden or unusual headaches, dizziness, or neck pain that is different from your normal
  • Osteoporosis or bone-thinning conditions, past spinal surgery, or implanted hardware
  • Blood-thinning medication or a bleeding disorder
  • Numbness, tingling, or weakness in your arms or legs

Based on that and the physical exam, we may adjust the plan, use a gentler approach, or recommend you see another provider first. A chiropractor who never says "not today" is one to be careful about. If you want the full picture of the first appointment, read what happens at your first chiropractic exam.

After a crash, extra care matters

If you are coming in after a car accident, we pay particular attention. Injured necks can behave differently from healthy ones, which is part of why the exam comes first and why a gentler approach may be chosen. Our whiplash guide and auto injury care page cover what that looks like.

Warning signs that mean call 911

After any kind of neck treatment, or really at any time, seek emergency care immediately if you have:

  • Sudden, severe headache or neck pain unlike anything you have felt before
  • Dizziness, double vision, or trouble seeing
  • Slurred speech, facial drooping, or trouble swallowing
  • Weakness or numbness on one side of the body

Those are not "wait and see" symptoms. They can be stroke signs, whatever the cause, so call right away.

A fair way to decide

Ask questions. Ask what the exam found, why an adjustment is being recommended, and what the alternatives are. Not sure whether you need to be seen at all? When to see a chiropractor versus waiting it out might help. And if you would simply like to talk first, our new patients page has the details, or you can get in touch.

Have questions before your first visit?

Call and ask them. We would much rather answer before you are on the table than after.
