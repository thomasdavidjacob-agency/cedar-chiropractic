When Should You See a Chiropractor vs. Wait It Out?

By the Cedar Chiropractic Team · 4 min read · Back to blog

You tweaked something shoveling gravel on Saturday. It is Wednesday and your back still grumbles when you stand up. Do you tough it out, or is this when to see a chiropractor? Here is how we think about it.

What is usually fine to watch for a few days

Bodies recover from plenty of things without help. A stiff neck after sleeping on the couch, sore muscles after a weekend of yard work, a back that is a little cranky after a long drive. If it is mild, it is getting better each day, and you can move around normally, giving it a few days of gentle activity is reasonable.

What "getting better" means matters here. Better means you are doing more than you were on Monday. Holding steady while you avoid everything that hurts is not the same thing.

Signs it is time for an exam

As a rough guide, think about booking a visit if:

  • The pain has not eased noticeably after a week or two
  • It keeps coming back in the same spot
  • It wakes you up at night or changes how you sleep
  • Pain, tingling, or numbness is traveling into an arm or leg
  • You are skipping things you normally do, like lifting a grandchild or turning to check your blind spot
  • You find yourself reaching for pain relievers every day just to get through work

None of that means something serious is wrong. It means an exam can tell you, instead of both of us guessing. Our spinal adjustments page and services overview show what care at Cedar can look like, and what to expect at your first chiropractic exam covers the first appointment.

After a car accident, do not wait for it to hurt

This is the one situation where the "give it a few days" rule works against you. Adrenaline can hide pain after a crash, and symptoms often show up a day or three later. Even if the other driver barely tapped your bumper on McLoughlin Blvd, getting checked early is sensible. We explain why in why pain shows up days after a car accident and whether you need a chiropractor after a minor fender-bender. Our auto injury page covers how those visits work with insurance. Your policy governs the details, so ask your agent if you are unsure.

From the front desk: you do not need a referral to book with us, and you do not need to be sure it is "bad enough." If you are asking the question, a phone call is free.

When the answer is the ER, not us

Some symptoms should not wait for any appointment. Call 911 or go to the ER for:

  • Loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness in the groin or inner thighs
  • Sudden weakness in an arm or leg, or trouble speaking or walking
  • Severe pain after a fall or crash
  • Chest pain, shortness of breath, or pain spreading into the jaw or left arm
  • The worst headache of your life, especially with a stiff neck or fever

If you are torn between the ER and a clinic after an accident, who to see after a crash lays out the difference. When in doubt, lean toward the ER.

Why people put it off

The reasons are usually the same. "It is not that bad." "I will lose an afternoon." "I do not want my back cracked." The first is fair until it is not, which is the point of the list above. The second is easy to check, because you can ask on the phone how long a first visit runs. The third is a conversation to have with the doctor; the exam does not have to end in an adjustment, and you can say what you are comfortable with.

A chiropractor is not the answer to everything

Some problems belong with your regular doctor or a specialist, and a good exam will tell you when that is the case. Chiropractic care is conservative and drug-free, and it fits a lot of spine and joint problems, but not all of them. If we think you need someone else, we will say so.
