How Traction, Decompression, and Rehab Fit Into Whiplash Recovery

By the Cedar Chiropractic Team · 4 min read · Back to blog

Patients sometimes arrive with a list of words they've picked up online. Traction, decompression, rehab, adjustments: all good words, though it isn't always clear how they fit together.

Here's the way we think about them after a whiplash-type injury. Think of it as three jobs, not three competing treatments.

Job one: calm things down

In the first stretch after a crash, a neck is usually irritated, guarded, and sore. Muscles are tight, joints are cranky, and discs may be getting squeezed because everything's clenched.

Cervical traction is a gentle, controlled stretch that can take pressure off the discs and the nerves that run between the bones of your neck. It's not a hard yank, and it's not a chiropractor pulling on your head. It's a measured, supported pull that you lie back and let happen. It may help settle things when stiffness and radiating pain are part of the picture.

Whether it's right for you depends on your exam. It isn't for everybody, and we'd rather tell you that than put you in a machine because it's on the menu.

Job two: restore motion

This is where adjustments usually come in. A joint that's stopped moving normally tends to keep irritating the muscles around it, and tight muscles keep the joint from moving. The cycle feeds itself. The goal is to interrupt it and get the neck turning, tilting, and nodding the way it did before the crash.

Job three: rebuild

People skip this part more than any other, usually because they feel better and stop coming. The small muscles deep in the neck and upper back are the ones that hold you steady, and after an injury they get weak and lazy. If they don't recover, the neck stays vulnerable.

Rehab exercises are how those muscles get their strength and coordination back. They're usually simple. A few minutes a day, done correctly, tends to matter more than an hour done sporadically. Shoulder-blade strengthening often comes into it, too. More on why this part matters in why rehab exercises matter for lasting relief.

One honest caveat before we go further. Nobody can tell you in advance how many weeks a whiplash recovery will take, and anyone who does is guessing. What we can do is re-check your neck as you go and adjust the plan to match what's actually changing.

What a traction session is like

The setup varies by device and by which part of your spine we're working on, but the general idea is simple. You're positioned comfortably and supported, and a gentle pull is applied for a set amount of time. It shouldn't hurt. If it does, tell us and we'll change something. Many people describe it as surprisingly relaxing. Our post on how cervical traction works goes deeper.

A good question to ask any provider who recommends it: why this for me, and how will we know if it's helping?

And where does decompression come in?

Spinal decompression is a close cousin of traction, usually used for the lower back (see lumbar traction and spinal decompression for low-back pain) and sometimes for the neck, where the goal is to ease pressure on a disc by gently, repeatedly stretching the spine. If your crash aggravated a disc, or if you've got pain, tingling, or numbness running into an arm or leg, it's something we might talk about. You can read the details on our spinal decompression page.

Any arm weakness that's getting worse, or numbness that spreads, needs prompt medical attention. Don't wait on it.

Who it isn't for

Traction isn't right for everyone. Certain conditions make it a poor choice, which is part of why the exam comes first and why we'd rather rule it out than use it by default. If we think you need imaging or a medical evaluation before going further, we'll tell you so.

Putting it together

Most plans aren't one thing. They move: calm things down, get the joints moving, then strengthen so it holds. The pace depends on you. If you're in Milwaukie, Oak Grove, Gladstone, or the Portland area and you want a plan built around your own exam, start at our auto injury page.
