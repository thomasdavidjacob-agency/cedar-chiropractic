The Neck-Headache Connection: When a Chiropractor for Headaches Makes Sense

By the Cedar Chiropractic Team · 5 min read · Back to blog

Not every headache starts in your head. If yours comes with a stiff neck, a tight band across the back of the skull, or pain that creeps up from your shoulders by mid-afternoon, the source may be the neck. People around Milwaukie, Oak Grove, and Gladstone ask us whether a chiropractor for headaches makes sense, and the honest answer is "it depends on what kind of headache you have," which is exactly what an exam is meant to sort out.

How the neck can set off a headache

The joints and muscles at the top of your spine share nerve pathways with parts of your head. When those joints get stiff or the small muscles at the base of the skull stay tight, the discomfort can be felt higher up: behind an eye, along one side of the head, or wrapping around like a hat that is one size too small. Clinicians sometimes call this a cervicogenic headache, meaning a headache that starts in the neck. Tension-type headaches, the common squeezing kind, often travel with tight neck and shoulder muscles too.

Long hours at a screen, a rough night on the wrong pillow, a long drive on I-205, or an old neck injury can all keep those tissues irritated.

Clues that the neck may be involved

  • The headache is on one side and starts at the base of the skull
  • Your neck feels stiff, or turning your head is limited or uncomfortable
  • Certain neck positions or a long stretch at your desk bring it on
  • Pressing on tender spots at the back of the head or neck reproduces it

None of that is a diagnosis. It is a reason to have your neck looked at by someone who can tell you what is going on.

What about migraines?

Migraines are a medical condition with their own patterns, and they are not something we diagnose or treat. Neck tension can overlap with migraine, and people often cannot tell the two apart on their own. If you have throbbing pain with nausea, light sensitivity, or visual changes before the pain, or if you have never had a headache evaluated, start with your primary care provider. An exam here can help sort out whether your neck seems to be part of the picture, and if something does not fit, we will point you back to your physician rather than stretch to make it fit.

Worth knowing: A short headache log helps at any appointment. Note when it starts, where it hurts, what you were doing, and what changed it. Bring it with you.

Go to the ER or call 911 first if you have

  • A sudden, severe headache, or the worst headache of your life
  • A headache with fever and a stiff neck
  • Weakness or numbness in the face, arm, or leg
  • Changes in vision, trouble speaking, confusion, or a seizure
  • A headache that follows a blow to the head, or one that keeps getting worse

These need medical attention before anything else. If a headache began after a collision, our guides on headaches after a car accident and concussion red flags cover what to watch for, and our auto injury page explains how that kind of care is set up.

What an exam looks at

We start with your history: when the headaches began, how often, where they hurt, and what you have already tried. Then we check how your neck moves, find the tender and restricted areas, look at your posture, and screen for signs that a nerve or something outside the neck needs a different kind of care. If you have never been to us before, what happens at your first chiropractic exam walks through the visit, and our new patient page covers the practical side.

How chiropractic care may fit in

When the exam points to the neck, care often focuses on the neck itself. Depending on what we find, a plan may include gentle spinal adjustments for stiff joints, cervical traction when it suits your neck, ultrasound and electrical muscle stimulation for tight, irritated muscles, and rehab exercises to build neck and upper-back support. These approaches may help some neck-related headaches. They are not a treatment for every headache, and we cannot promise a result or a set number of visits.
