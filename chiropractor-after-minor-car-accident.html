Do You Really Need a Chiropractor After a Minor Fender-Bender?

By the Cedar Chiropractic Team · 4 min read · Back to blog

"It was barely a tap. Do I really need to come in?" We hear some version of that nearly every week, and the honest answer is: maybe not. But you can't know that from how the car looks.

Why a small bump can still hurt

Think about the grocery store parking lot. Someone rolls into your bumper at five or ten miles an hour, you both get out, look at the paint, shrug. Meanwhile your head and neck just got a quick, jerky shove that nobody saw. Bumpers are designed to absorb impact at low speeds. They protect the car. They don't do much for the person in the seat.

So a crash can leave a car with a scuff and a body with a strained neck. The damage estimate and the injury don't always match.

Who probably doesn't need to rush in

Here's the part most clinic websites skip. If you feel completely normal, you've got full range of motion, you're sleeping fine, and nothing's changed after a few days, you may be perfectly okay. Plenty of people are. We'd never tell someone with no symptoms that they need weeks of care.

What we would say is that "no symptoms right now" and "no injury" aren't the same thing. Soft-tissue injuries often take a day or three to show up. Watch yourself for the first week.

Signs it's worth getting checked

  • Your neck or back feels tighter than it did before the accident
  • You've got a new headache that won't quite go away
  • Turning your head to merge on I-205 hurts or feels limited
  • You're waking up sore, or can't get comfortable at night
  • Your shoulder, arm, or hip feels off in a way you can't pin down

Any one of those is a reason to book an exam. And if you have severe headache, confusion, numbness, or weakness, skip us and go to the ER.

Seeing it coming versus not

If you spot a crash a second early, you tend to brace without thinking: hands tight on the wheel, shoulders up, head pressed back. If you're glancing at the mirror or the map and it comes out of nowhere, your muscles get caught off guard. A neck that's relaxed and unprepared is often thought to be more vulnerable, though it varies a lot from person to person and nobody can predict who will end up sore.

Which way your head was turned matters too. A glance over the shoulder at the moment of impact puts the neck in a more awkward spot than facing straight ahead.

None of this is a reason to panic. It's a reason not to rely on "the damage was small" as your whole assessment.

From the front desk: A lot of people hold off because they don't want to "make a thing" of a small accident. An exam isn't making a thing of it. It's just finding out where you stand, and it gives you a record dated close to the crash if symptoms do show up later.

What an exam involves

We talk through what happened, check how your spine moves, feel for tight or tender spots, and look for anything that suggests a nerve is involved. If the exam turns up something that needs a different kind of care, we'll say so. If it turns up the kind of strain chiropractic is good at, we'll lay out a plan, which might include gentle adjustments and soft-tissue work, and you decide what to do with it.

What about the cost?

Oregon auto policies include personal injury protection (PIP), which is no-fault, so it can apply even when the crash wasn't your fault and even when it was minor. We bill PIP directly, so many people start with little or nothing out of pocket. Your own policy governs what's covered, so call your insurer or agent to confirm. That's what the exam is for. If nothing's wrong, you'll know. If something is, you'll know that too.
