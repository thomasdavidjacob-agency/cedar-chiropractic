Massage & Soft Tissue

Hands-on soft-tissue work that releases tight muscles and complements your adjustments.

Home / Services / Massage & Soft Tissue

What soft-tissue therapy is

Your spine is held in place and moved by muscles, tendons, and fascia — the connective tissue that wraps them. When those tissues are tight, knotted, or guarding after an injury, they can pull on joints and keep pain going. Soft-tissue therapy, including therapeutic massage and myofascial release, works directly on those tissues to ease tension and help them move freely again.

This isn’t a spa service. It’s focused, clinical work aimed at the areas your exam shows are contributing to your pain, and it’s designed to complement your adjustments.

What it’s commonly used for

  • Tight, sore muscles in the neck, shoulders, and back
  • Upper-back and shoulder tension from long hours at a desk or screen
  • Muscle guarding and stiffness after an injury
  • Loosening tissue before an adjustment so it’s more comfortable

What a visit feels like

Soft-tissue work is usually part of a broader visit. Pressure is matched to you — firm enough to be useful, never more than you’re comfortable with — and you can ask for lighter or firmer at any time. Some tender spots may feel sore while they’re being worked on, and a little residual soreness the next day is normal.

Posture and desk strain

If your tension comes from how you sit and work, easing the muscles is only half the job. We also offer posture assessments and workspace guidance, and simple exercises to support better habits — see rehab & corrective exercise.

Who it may not be right for

Hands-on work may need to be modified or avoided over a recent fracture, an open wound or skin infection, a suspected blood clot, or areas affected by certain other medical conditions. Let us know your health history and any medications, such as blood thinners, and we’ll adapt the approach.

Common Questions

Massage & Soft Tissue: questions we hear

Is this the same as a spa massage?

No. It’s clinical soft-tissue work aimed at the muscles your exam shows are contributing to your pain, and it’s designed to complement your chiropractic care.

How much pressure will you use?

As much as is useful and no more than you’re comfortable with. You can ask for lighter or firmer pressure at any time.

Can soft-tissue work help desk-related neck and shoulder tension?

It can help ease that tension. For longer-lasting change, we usually pair it with posture and workspace guidance and a few simple exercises.
