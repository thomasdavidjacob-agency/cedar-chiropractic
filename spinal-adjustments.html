Spinal Adjustments

Precise, gentle adjustments that may help restore movement and ease back and neck pain — the foundation of chiropractic care at Cedar.

Home / Services / Spinal Adjustments

What a spinal adjustment is

An adjustment — also called spinal manipulation — is a precise, controlled movement applied by hand to a joint in the spine that isn’t moving the way it should. The aim is to restore natural motion, ease pressure on the nerves and tissue around that joint, and help the area move more comfortably again.

Adjustments are the foundation of chiropractic care, but at Cedar they’re usually one part of a plan rather than the whole plan. Dr. Fred Seater chooses each therapy for your case, not from a package.

What it’s commonly used for

Patients in Milwaukie and the surrounding area often come in for:

  • Neck pain and stiffness, including stiffness that shows up after a car accident
  • Mid- and lower-back pain, whether it came on suddenly or has lingered for months
  • Tension headaches that trace back to the neck and upper back
  • Limited range of motion — trouble turning your head, bending, or reaching
  • Ongoing maintenance once the pain that brought you in has settled

Every body is different, so we’ll tell you honestly whether adjustments are likely to help in your situation.

What a visit feels like

Before any adjustment, Dr. Fred examines you, explains what he’s found, and walks you through what he recommends. You stay comfortably clothed and lie or sit on a padded treatment table. The adjustment itself is quick.

Some people hear a soft pop. That’s gas being released from the joint — not bones “cracking.” Techniques are matched to your comfort level, and gentler, low-force options are available if you prefer them. Mild soreness for a day or so afterward can happen, a little like the day after a new workout. If anything doesn’t feel right, tell us and we’ll stop.

Who it may not be right for

Adjustments aren’t appropriate for everyone. Certain conditions — for example a recent fracture, significant osteoporosis, some inflammatory or circulatory conditions, or recent spinal surgery — may call for a different approach or a referral back to your physician. That’s one reason every plan starts with a thorough history and exam. If adjustments aren’t the right fit for you, we’ll say so and talk through what may be.

How it fits with other therapies

Adjustments often work best alongside care for the muscles, discs, and stability around the joint:

  • Massage & Soft TissueLoosens tight muscles before and after an adjustment
  • Ultrasound & E-StimEases spasm and supports soft-tissue healing
  • Spinal DecompressionFor disc-related back and leg pain
  • Rehab & Corrective ExerciseBuilds the strength that helps results last

Common Questions

Spinal Adjustments: questions we hear

Does a chiropractic adjustment hurt?

Most patients find adjustments comfortable. Some feel mild soreness for a day or so afterward. Techniques are matched to your comfort level, and gentler low-force options are available — just tell us what you prefer.

What is the popping sound during an adjustment?

It’s gas being released from the fluid inside the joint as it moves — the same thing that happens when you crack your knuckles. It isn’t bones cracking, and not every adjustment makes a sound.

Will I be adjusted at every visit?

Not necessarily. Your plan is based on your exam, and some visits may focus more on soft-tissue work, traction, or exercise. Dr. Fred will explain what he recommends and why.

Can I get adjusted soon after a car accident?

Possibly. We examine you first, and depending on what we find we may start with gentler therapies before adjusting. Being seen sooner rather than later also helps document your injury for your claim.
