Posture at Your Desk: Small Fixes That Prevent Big Pain

By the Cedar Chiropractic Team · 5 min read · Back to blog

The best desk posture is the one you do not hold for very long. That surprises people, because we all picture a rigid, shoulders-back, perfectly upright worker. Real comfort at a desk comes from a setup that fits you and from changing position often. Whether you work in an office in Portland or at a kitchen counter in Gladstone, here are practical fixes that are easy to try this week.

Start with the screen

Most neck strain at a desk traces back to where the screen is. Aim to have the top of the display around eye level, about an arm's length away, directly in front of you rather than off to one side. If you use a laptop for long stretches, the screen and keyboard are stuck together, so lifting the screen means you will want a separate keyboard. A stack of sturdy books under the laptop works fine as a stand. You do not need to buy anything special.

Sort out the chair and your feet

  • Seat height: Adjust so your hips are about level with, or slightly above, your knees.
  • Feet: Keep them flat on the floor. If they dangle, a sturdy box or a rolled towel can serve as a footrest.
  • Lower back: Sit back so your lower back is supported. A small rolled towel can fill the gap if your chair lacks support.
  • Arms: Let your elbows rest close to your sides at roughly a right angle, with shoulders relaxed rather than hiked up.

Keyboard, mouse, and phone

Keep the keyboard and mouse close enough that you are not reaching. Reaching pulls the shoulder forward and tenses the neck. If you spend a lot of time on calls, use speaker or a headset rather than pinching the phone between ear and shoulder. If you use a second monitor, put the one you look at most directly in front of you.

Move more than you think you need to

Even a good setup gets tiring if you freeze in it. A few habits that fit into a normal day:

  • Stand up, walk, or stretch for a minute or two every so often, and change position when you notice you have been still a while.
  • Take calls standing or pacing when you can.
  • Roll your shoulders back and down, and gently turn your head side to side within a comfortable range.
  • Look away from the screen into the distance now and then to rest your eyes.

If any movement increases pain, stop and have it checked rather than push through.

Worth knowing: Posture is not a test you pass or fail. A body that shifts and moves through the day usually feels better than one held still in a "perfect" position.

When pain is telling you something

Tips like these are general, and they do not replace an exam. If you have neck or back pain that keeps coming back, pain that wakes you at night, or symptoms that spread, get it looked at. Seek urgent medical care, or call 911 for severe or sudden symptoms, if you have numbness or weakness in an arm or leg, loss of bladder or bowel control, a sudden severe headache, chest pain, or pain following a fall or crash.
