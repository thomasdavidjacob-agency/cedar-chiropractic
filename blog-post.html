5 desk stretches to beat lower-back pain

By Dr. Fred · 6 min read · Back to blog

Featured image

If you spend your days at a desk, your lower back is quietly paying the price. The good news? A few simple stretches, done regularly, can undo much of the damage — no gym required.

Sitting for hours shortens the hip flexors, weakens the core, and loads the lumbar spine in ways it wasn't designed for. Over time, that adds up to the stiffness and aching so many of our patients describe. Here are five of Dr. Fred's favorite desk-friendly stretches to keep you moving well.

1. Seated cat-cow

Sit tall with feet flat. On an inhale, arch your back and lift your chest; on an exhale, round your spine and tuck your chin. Flow between the two for 30 seconds to gently mobilize the whole spine.

2. Seated figure-four

Cross one ankle over the opposite knee and hinge forward slightly until you feel a stretch in the glute. Hold for 20–30 seconds each side. This releases tension that often refers pain into the lower back.

Dr. Fred's tip: Set a reminder to stand and move for two minutes every hour. Motion is lotion for your spine — frequency matters more than intensity.

3. Standing hip flexor stretch

Step one foot back into a short lunge, tuck your pelvis under, and reach the same-side arm overhead. You'll feel the front of the hip open up — a key fix for desk-bound backs.

4. Doorway chest opener

Place your forearms on a doorframe and step through gently. Rounded shoulders from typing pull the whole posture forward; this counteracts it and takes strain off the mid and lower back.

5. Standing back extension

Place your hands on your lower back and gently lean backward, looking slightly up. Hold for a few seconds and repeat 5–10 times to reverse hours of forward flexion.

When to see a chiropractor

Stretching helps, but if your pain lingers beyond a couple of weeks, radiates down a leg, or keeps returning, it's worth getting checked. A proper exam can pinpoint the cause and get you a plan that actually sticks.
