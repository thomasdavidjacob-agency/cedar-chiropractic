Spinal Decompression

A non-surgical, drug-free therapy that gently stretches the spine to take pressure off discs and nerves.

Home / Services / Spinal Decompression

What spinal decompression is

Spinal decompression is a slow, controlled stretch of the spine. The goal is to create a little more space between the vertebrae, easing pressure on the discs that cushion them and on the nerves nearby. When a disc is bulging or herniated and pressing on a nerve, taking some of that pressure off may ease the pain that comes with it.

It’s non-surgical and doesn’t involve medication. For many people it’s worth exploring before — or alongside a conversation with their physician about — more invasive options.

What it’s commonly used for

  • Herniated or bulging discs
  • Sciatica — pain, numbness, or tingling that runs from the low back into the leg
  • Chronic lower-back pain, especially when it’s disc-related
  • Back pain that hasn’t settled with rest and time

What a visit feels like

After your exam, you’re positioned comfortably on the treatment table and the stretch is applied gradually. Most people describe it as a gentle pulling sensation rather than pain, and many simply rest during the session. We check in with you throughout, and the stretch can be eased or stopped at any time.

Some patients feel relief fairly quickly; for others, change is gradual. Dr. Fred will give you an honest estimate after your exam rather than a one-size-fits-all number of visits.

Who it may not be right for

Decompression isn’t a fit for every back. It may not be appropriate during pregnancy, after recent spinal surgery or with spinal hardware, with a recent fracture or significant osteoporosis, or with certain other medical conditions. We screen for these in the exam, and if decompression isn’t right for you, we’ll tell you and suggest what may be — including a referral to your physician when that’s the better path.

How it fits with other therapies

Decompression and traction are closely related — both aim to relieve pressure on discs — and we’ll recommend whichever suits your case. Decompression is often paired with:

  • Cervical & Lumbar TractionA closely related way to ease disc pressure
  • Spinal AdjustmentsRestores motion in stiff joints
  • Rehab & Corrective ExerciseStrengthens the core that supports your spine
  • Ultrasound & E-StimCalms the muscle spasm that often comes with disc pain

Back or leg pain since a crash? The forces in a collision can aggravate the discs in your lower back, and symptoms sometimes appear days later. Common Questions

Spinal Decompression: questions we hear

Is spinal decompression surgery?

No. It’s a non-surgical, drug-free therapy — a controlled stretch applied while you rest on a treatment table. There are no incisions, injections, or medication involved.

Does spinal decompression hurt?

Most patients describe a gentle pulling or stretching sensation. We check in throughout, and the stretch can be eased or stopped at any time if you’re uncomfortable.

Will decompression fix my herniated disc?

We can’t promise a specific result — no honest provider can. The aim is to relieve pressure on the disc and nerve so your pain eases and you can move better. After your exam, Dr. Fred will tell you plainly whether he thinks it’s worth trying.

How is decompression different from traction?

They’re closely related — both gently stretch the spine to take pressure off discs and nerves. Which one we recommend depends on where your problem is and what your exam shows.
