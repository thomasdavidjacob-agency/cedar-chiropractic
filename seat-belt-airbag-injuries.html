Seat Belt and Airbag Soft-Tissue Injuries After a Crash

By the Cedar Chiropractic Team · 5 min read · Back to blog

"The seat belt left a mark, but that's what it's for." True, and a bruise across your shoulder and chest is common. It's also worth a closer look. A seat belt injury after a car accident can be nothing more than a sore strip of skin, or it can sit on top of something that needs a medical exam.

First, the part that can't wait

Seat belts and airbags save lives, and the force they manage is large. If any of these show up after a crash, go to the ER or call 911 instead of booking a chiropractic visit:

  • Chest pain or trouble breathing
  • Belly pain, swelling, or a hard, tender abdomen where the lap belt sat
  • A severe headache, or a headache that keeps getting worse
  • Confusion or unusual drowsiness
  • Numbness or weakness in an arm or leg
  • Loss of bladder control

Pain over the ribs or breastbone also deserves a medical look, since a sore chest wall and a broken rib can feel alike. A chiropractor can't see that on the outside, and we won't guess at it.

What the belt does to your body

In a sudden stop, the belt holds your hips and one shoulder while the rest of you keeps moving. The strap takes the load across the collarbone, the chest and the lower belly. The soft tissue under it, meaning muscle, tendon and ligament, gets pulled and compressed. Bruising is the visible part. Underneath, people often feel:

  • A sore, tight band from the shoulder diagonally across the chest
  • Pain when twisting, reaching for the back seat, or taking a deep breath
  • Soreness in the neck on the side the belt crossed
  • Low-back stiffness from the lap portion and from the jolt itself

Airbags are a different kind of hit

An airbag goes off in a fraction of a second, and it hits hard. People report sore wrists and forearms from being thrown against the wheel, scrapes on the face and arms, and a stiff neck. Ringing ears and a foggy head happen too. If you're feeling off mentally after the bag deployed, read our notes on concussion red flags after a car accident before you decide it's just stress.

Why people shrug it off

Picture someone pulling out of a parking lot in Oak Grove after a fender-bender. The belt locked, the airbag stayed in, there's a red line across the collarbone. They'd feel silly seeing a doctor for that. Then the next morning the shoulder won't lift and the lower back is tight. Bruises color up and the pain moves around. We've covered muscle spasms after a car accident and low-back pain after a crash, and both often begin as "I'm just sore."

Small details help at the visit

You don't need a perfect memory of the crash. It helps to know roughly which seat you were in, whether you saw it coming, and whether your head or chest hit anything. Mention the bruise too, and where it is, since the line of a seat belt tells us a lot about how the force moved through you. Take a photo of the bruise now if you can, because it will change color over the next several days. If you've already been seen at urgent care or the ER, bring whatever paperwork they gave you.

What a chiropractic exam can do

We'll ask how the crash happened, where the belt crossed you, and whether the airbag went off. Then we look at how your neck, shoulder, ribs and spine move. If the picture says soft tissue and joint irritation, care may include spinal adjustments where appropriate, ultrasound and electrical stimulation to calm irritated tissue, and rehab exercises once you're ready for them. If the picture says imaging or a medical doctor, we'll say so plainly.

Worth knowing: Oregon auto policies include personal injury protection (PIP), and Cedar bills PIP directly. Your policy governs what applies to you, so ask your agent about the details. This is general information and not legal advice. Our auto injury page explains the process, and what to do in the first 72 hours is a good checklist if the crash was recent.
